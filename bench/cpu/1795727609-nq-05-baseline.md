# nq: 0.5× llama.cpp baseline — prefill parity, decode 0.35×, and the bandwidth ceiling

Criterion: **T1** (decode) and **T2** (turn latency), baseline only. This receipt fixes the
campaign's starting point on the OVH box: the committed tree at `8a740f0f…` against llama.cpp
`049326a00`, same host, same 1,827-position context, 128 decode tokens. It measures; it changes
nothing. The result is **7.95 s** per measured turn against llama's **5.36 s** (1.48× slower):
prefill is at parity (3.90 s against 3.96 s), decode is **0.35×** llama (31.6 against 91.6 tok/s).
The 0.5× campaign target is 2.68 s per turn, so the tree is **2.97× short**, and the bandwidth
arithmetic below refuses that target at the current KV carrier before any kernel is written.

Grades: `measured` for every wall, rate, phase mean and the STREAM result; `model` for the ratios,
the `1/(1 − s)` ceilings, the bytes-per-token arithmetic and the two decode ceilings. Nothing is
`proved` or `tested` here.

Prefill is 1.016× llama's rate; decode is 0.345×. The turn (prefill + decode, both at 1,827

`attn.core` is the dominant term at 43.0% and its ceiling is 1.754×: a free attention kernel alone
takes decode only to 16.6 ms/token (60 tok/s), still 0.65× llama. The other named leaves carry
10–25% each; the 5.8× decode gap is a sum over several kernels, not one dominant term. `pool.wait`
at 19.6% is an overlay, not additive: it says the work is cut fine enough that scheduling costs
about a fifth of the wall.

## Two decode ceilings

A decode token reads, per token:

| read | bytes |
|---|---:|
| pack factors (196 linears, embedding lookup excluded) | 53,808,384 |
| q8 `output.weight` (151,936 × 1,024, Q8_0) | 165,306,368 |
| KV at 1,827 positions × 229,376 B/position | 419,069,952 |
| **total** | **638,184,704** |

Measured STREAM (`tools/membw.c`, 128 MiB × 25 passes × 8 threads): **100.74 GB/s** mean,
112.72 GB/s best pass.

```text
bandwidth ceiling = 100.74 GB/s ÷ 638.2 MB/token = 157.8 tok/s   (mean)
                    112.72 GB/s ÷ 638.2 MB/token = 176.6 tok/s  (best pass)
target            = 183.1 tok/s (0.5 × llama's 91.55)
```

At the current carrier the target is **above the measured bandwidth ceiling**: 183.1 is 116% of the
mean and 104% of the best pass. The tree runs at 31.6 tok/s, 20.0 GB/s, 20% of the measured mean —
so the immediate decode gap is instruction rate, not bandwidth, but the target is not reachable by
instruction rate alone. With an F16 KV carrier (2 B/value, llama's carrier) the KV read halves to
209.5 MB and the total to 428.6 MB: the mean ceiling becomes **235.0 tok/s**, and the 183.1 target
is 78% of it. The bytes-per-token reduction is part of the 0.5× route, not an optimisation after
it. The issue-rate bound (instructions per weight × physical cores × clock × IPC) is not yet
counted; it is the next artifact, from the emitted LLVM and assembly of both kernels.

For comparison, llama's per-token read is about 881 MB of F16 weights (embedding lookup excluded)
plus 209.5 MB of KV = 1,090.5 MB; at 91.55 tok/s that is 99.8 GB/s, essentially the measured mean.
llama's decode is bandwidth-bound at this context; ours is not, and that is the gap the kernels must
close.

## Refusals

1. **"The tree is at 0.5×."** Refused: 7.95 s against 5.36 s is 1.48× slower; 2.97× short of the
   2.68 s target.
2. **"Prefill is the problem."** Refused: prefill is 468 tok/s against llama's 461, 1.016×; decode
   is 0.345× and owns 50.9% of our turn.
3. **"One kernel closes decode."** Refused: the dominant leaf `attn.core` has a 1.754× ceiling; the
   turn needs 2.97×. The gap is distributed across `attn.core` 43.0%, `ffn` 25.2%, `head` 10.8%,
   `proj_in` 10.5%, `proj_out` 7.4%.
4. **"The 0.5× decode target is reachable at the current KV carrier."** Refused: the bandwidth
   ceiling is 157.8 tok/s (mean) against a 183.1 tok/s target. An F16 KV carrier moves the ceiling
   to 235.0 tok/s; the carrier change is a precondition.
5. **"This names the laptop."** Refused: box only. The laptop (i5-10210U, 15 W, 6 MB L3) has not
   run this fixture and its bandwidth is not measured.

## Limits

- 0.6B, 1,827 positions, q8 head, 0.977441 b/w pack. Not the 7B, not 200k, not the 1.00 b/w
  product. The dense KV at 200k is 45.9 GB on this carrier and is not reachable.
- The issue-rate bound is not counted; only bandwidth is. A kernel change still needs the phase
  gate and a paired wall.
- `pool.wait` is an overlay and is not summed into the leaves.
- The box is CPU-tier evidence; no timed result here names the laptop.
