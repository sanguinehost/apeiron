# NanoQuant at 1.15 bit per weight: stored rate, distortion against the shipped factor, and the DRAM bytes

Status: `measured` unless a line says otherwise. Machine: vast.ai `60770801`, i5-13400F
(Raptor Lake, 16 vCPU, `avx_vnni`, 15.7 GB), Ubuntu 24.04 container. Source: `9f9c0d02` plus the
working-tree paths this branch carries (`src/engine/kern/matmat.rs`, `src/learn/nanoquant.rs`,
`tools/nq_*.py`); the KV work is not involved. Teacher `Qwen3-0.6B-Base-F16.gguf`; calibration
`c6x29`, the 112 Grams of 6×29 chunks; rows: layers 0, 26 and 27 × `ffn_down`, `ffn_gate`,
`ffn_up`, `attn_output`.

The question this answers: the card advertised "an exact 1.18 bit-per-weight pipeline, with 3.8
times lower DRAM traffic than llama.cpp Q4_K_M and 3.5 to 32.7 times lower reconstruction
distortion than NanoQuant". Each of the three numbers is checked here against the artifact it
names.

The shipped factor is SamsungLabs/NanoQuant's Qwen3-0.6B export. Its reconstruction is decoded from

## 1. The fitted artifact

Twelve `nq_fit` runs, `APEIRON_NQ_CMP=nqref` (score against the shipped factor inside the fit),
`APEIRON_NQ_BITS_PPM=1180000`, `APEIRON_NQ_ITERS=400`, `APEIRON_THREADS=16`, seed 0:

```
APEIRON_NQ_MODEL=Qwen3-0.6B-Base-F16.gguf APEIRON_NQ_CACHE=gatecode-tok APEIRON_NQ_TAG=118 \
APEIRON_NQ_LAYERS=<l> APEIRON_NQ_KIND=<kind> APEIRON_NQ_CMP=nqref APEIRON_THREADS=16 \
APEIRON_NQ_BITS_PPM=1180000 APEIRON_NQ_ITERS=400 \
  cargo test --release --test learn_nanoquant nq_fit -- --ignored --exact --nocapture
```

`APEIRON_NQ_BITS_PPM` sets the rank through `rank_for_bits(o, n, ppm, 32)`, which rounds down to a
multiple of 32. The rate the fit prints is `stored_bits(o, n, r) / (o·n)` from
`src/learn/nanoquant.rs`, i.e. exactly the packed payload's bits, not a budget.

| shape | rank | stored b/w |
|---|---:|---:|
| 1024 × 3072 (`down`) and 3072 × 1024 (`gate`, `up`) | 864 | 1.1458 |
| 1024 × 2048 (`attn_output`) | 768 | 1.1484 |

The pack the engine reads is the authority: `tools/nq_pack_factors.py` wrote the twelve sections to
`model.nqp` in **4,978,784 B**, **1.1510 b/w on 34,603,008 weights**. That count includes the
`s_mid` vector (stored as 1), which the fit's own `stored_bits` does not.

The shipped factor on the same twelve rows stores **1.166 b/w** row-weighted (0.680–1.391 per row),
so the in-tree pack is 1.3% below it in rate, not above. Per-row rates are in the table below: the
shipped export allocates rank per row, and layer-26 `gate` gets the least (0.680 b/w).

## 2. Reconstruction distortion, twelve rows

Both sides are scored with the same metric on the same Gram: `relH_E = tr(E·H·Eᵀ)/tr(W·H·Wᵀ)`
and `relH = sqrt(relH_E) = ‖W − Ŵ‖_H/‖W‖_H`, the essay's definition. `H` is the `c6x29` input
Gram. The ratio column is `relH(fit) / relH(shipped)`: below 1 is the in-tree fit's lower error.

|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|

| 0 | `gate` | 1.1458 | 21.8598% | 1.391 | 20.0858% | 1.088 |

| 26 | `up` | 1.1458 | 33.5297% | 1.140 | 38.9586% | 0.861 |

Command: `python3 tools/nq_relh.py --model Qwen3-0.6B-Base-F16.gguf --cache gatecode-tok
--layers 0,26,27 --kinds down,gate,up,o --ref nqref --fit nq118 --csv relh-118.csv`.

**Aggregate: twelve rows, geometric mean norm ratio 0.822, min 0.450, max 1.088; the in-tree fit
is lower on 10 of 12.** At 1.151 b/w against the shipped factor's 1.166, its reconstruction error
is 18% lower on average, and 1.04–1.09× higher on layer-0 `gate` and `attn_output`.

The fit's own log prints `relH` as the *energy* fraction `tr(EHEᵀ)/tr(WHWᵀ)`, the square of the
norm; `tools/nq_relh.py` prints both and this table uses the norm. A ratio of energy fractions is
the square of a ratio of norms, so the two cannot be mixed in one comparison.

The shipped factor's rank allocation is not the in-tree one, so a row-by-row comparison mixes two
rate policies. The aggregate above is the honest summary; the rows where the in-tree fit loses
(layer-0 `gate` and `attn_output`) are rows where the shipped factor spends more rank
(1.391 and 1.366 b/w against 1.151).

### The served form of the same fits

The `.i64` dump is the fit's f64 reconstruction. The pack the engine reads stores `s_pre`/`s_post`
as binary16 and `s_mid` as 1 (`tools/nq_pack_factors.py`), which adds a small scale error. The
twelve rows were re-scored from that packed form (`--fit nqpack`, `relh-118-served.csv`):

**The packed form's largest change to any row's norm is 0.000086**, so the table above is also the
served artifact's: geometric mean 0.822, min 0.450, max 1.088, lower on 10 of 12. The bf16 scales
and the dropped `s_mid` are below the fifth decimal on these rows.

## 3. DRAM bytes, at four scopes

`tools/nq_bits.py` reads each GGUF's directory and sums the payload bytes of the tensors a pack
replaces; the block table is checked against each file's extent before anything prints. Tensors:
the five kinds a pack covers, `ffn_down`, `ffn_gate`, `ffn_up`, `attn_output`, `attn_qkv`
(q/k/v in the dense images), 440,401,920 weights.

| scope | ours | Q4_K_M | shipped NanoQuant | ours vs Q4_K_M |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| the five replaced kinds, at the served rates (qkv's 928 rank, 1.1559 b/w) | 63.43 MB | 262.86 MB | 56.59 MB | **4.14× fewer** |
| the four kinds measured row by row (323.0M weights) | 46.47 MB | 193.02 MB | — | **4.15× fewer** |
| whole payload (linears + F16 skeleton) | 374.86 MB | 390.75 MB | 387.47 MB | 1.04× fewer |
| per token, weights read once, tied head | 374.86 MB | 386.71 MB | 221.90 MB | 1.03× fewer |

Grades: the per-file bytes and the twelve-section pack are `measured` (`nq_bits.py`,
`nq_pack_factors.py`). The 63.43 MB total carries 16.97 MB of `attn_qkv` at 1.1559 b/w — its rank
at the same budget is 928 on 4096 × 1024, and that row was not fitted in this campaign, so the
five-kind total is `model`; the four-kind row applies the measured 12-section rate (1.1510) to 28
layers, whose shapes are identical, and its Q4_K_M side is measured tensor by tensor. The
per-token row is `model`: it adds the head each image reads per token at batch 1, no KV cache, no
activations.

The shipped image stores `attn_qkv` as one fused factor. The converter the engine serves splits it
into q, k and v sections that duplicate the shared `V`, `s_pre`, `s_mid` and index arrays, which is
6.77 MB on this model: the README's converted `nq06/model.nqp` is 63,370,960 B against the image's
56,593,520 B. On that **served** pack the comparison is 63.43 MB against 63.37 MB, the same rate to
0.1% — this pipeline is not a byte win over NanoQuant's own pack, only over Q4_K_M and over the
shipped factor's distortion on the sampled rows.

Three facts the table exposes:

- **Q4_K_M is not 4.50 b/w on these tensors.** `ffn_down` is Q6_K in 14 of 28 layers and Q4_K in
  the other 14, and `attn_v` is Q6_K in 14 of 28; the rest of the projections are Q4_K. The
  measured mean over the five kinds is 4.775 b/w, so the card's 4.50/1.18 = 3.81 was
  conservative.
- **The shipped factor is not smaller than Q4_K_M.** It stores the projections at 1.028 b/w but
  stores `token_embd` *and* an untied `output.weight`, both Q8_0, 165.3 MB each: 330.6 MB of the
  387.5 MB payload. The file is 3.2 MB smaller than Q4_K_M's, not 4× smaller.
- **The tied F16 head is the served path's dominant read.** `Engine::lm_head` returns
  `output.weight` when the directory has one and `token_embd.weight` otherwise
  (`src/engine/step.rs:1231`). The F16 teacher has neither an output tensor nor any compression of
  the embedding, so a pack built on it reads 311.16 MB of head per token against the 63.43 MB of
  projections. That is why the projection ratio (4.14×) and the per-token ratio (1.03×) differ by
  a factor of four. Storing the head at Q8_0, which the engine's head path already supports, would
  put the per-token total at 229.00 MB, 1.69× fewer than Q4_K_M; at Q6_K it would be 191.32 MB,
  2.02×. Neither is built here.

The card's sentence is about "DRAM traffic". At batch 1 the only honest whole-model reading of
that phrase is the per-token row, and there the number is 1.03×, not 3.8×. The 4.14–4.15× is real
but it is the projection tensors' bytes, and it is what the pack controls.

## 4. Where the card's numbers came from, and what replaces them

The essay behind the card (`lrw.com/drafts/one-bit-quantization/post.src.md`) puts NanoQuant's
reconstruction floor at a flat 28.45% and the in-tree result at 8.10% and 0.87%, and the card
divides: 28.45/8.10 = 3.51 and 28.45/0.87 = 32.70. The 8.10% and 0.87% are the removed E8 fold
fit, not this ADMM: `src/learn/fold/mod.rs` says "The E8 fold fit that gave the module its name is
removed", and only the scale quantisers and eigendecomposition remain.

The shipped 0.6B factor's own norm error on these twelve rows is 20.1–52.3%, not 28.45%, and its
rate is 1.166 b/w over them (1.028 over all 28 layers), not 1.18. The in-tree pack's norm error is
0.45–1.09× the shipped factor's on the same rows, lower on ten of twelve and 18% lower on average.
It is not 3.5× lower, let alone 32.7×.

Measured replacement for the card:

> An exact 1.15 bit-per-weight pipeline: 4.1 times fewer bytes than llama.cpp's Q4_K_M across the
> projections it replaces, and, at a slightly lower rate on the same rows (1.151 against 1.166 bit
> per weight), 18% lower reconstruction error on average than the shipped NanoQuant factor.

"Exact" is the byte count: the exported `model.nqp` is 4,978,784 B, 1.1510 b/w over the
34,603,008 weights it replaces. "18% on average" is the geometric mean of the twelve norm ratios;
lower on ten rows, 1.04–1.09× higher on the other two (§2).

## 5. Speed

The same twelve fits, 400 iterations each, release build, `APEIRON_THREADS=16` (ten pool
participants: six P-cores with the caller plus four E-cores):

| row | wall |
|---|---:|
| `down`, `gate` or `up`, 864 rank | 161.8–174.7 s (one layer-27 `down` at 391.1 s on the shared box) |
| `attn_output`, 768 rank | 93.8–113.1 s |

The pre-fix slope was `288.1 s / 10 = 28.8 s` per iteration on the Broadwell box (the routing cliff
sent every `k = r` projection to the per-column reference), so 400 iterations at that slope is
11.5 ks. 174.7 s is **66×**; the 4.19× routing fix, the 1.18–1.40× half-Gram and the 10.7× host
multiply to 53–63×, and the rest is setup. The 66× crosses two hosts, so it is an arithmetic
projection over measured slopes (`model`), not a measured ratio; the same-host measurement is the
back-to-back 4.19×.

The iteration budget is the lever that changes the artifact. On layer-0 `down`, against the same
shipped factor: T=200 83.8 s, 45.02% norm (1.3% above the 400-iteration 44.42%, 1.004× worse than
the shipped 44.85%); T=50 23.2 s, 47.70% (7.4% above). The T=200 row is 137× the pre-fix
400-iteration projection; that ratio, like the 66×, crosses hosts and is `model`. A 112-linear pack
at T=200 is `28 × (3 × 83.8 + 56.5) ≈ 2.4 h` on this box, and at T=400 the twelve fits here average
167 s, so `112 × 167 s ≈ 5.2 h`.

## 6. What this does not establish

- **A 28-layer pack and its perplexity.** The twelve rows are a rate/distortion sample, not a
  model. No full pack, no KL, no PPL on this model. The 8B pack's PPL receipts
  (`bench/cpu/1794653685-nq8b-residual-ppl.md`) are a different model, rate and artifact.
- **The half-Gram's refinement.** `x_update` now calls `syrk_q32` instead of `gemm`; the two are
  equal bit for bit in `src/engine/kern/limb.rs::tests::syrk_matches_gemm_bit_for_bit` (`tested`),
  and every fit pair reports the same `admm_loss`, but no corpus theorem refines
  `kern.limb.syrk_q32`. That kernel is extracted and outside the bridged surface.
- **Engine throughput.** No `serve/` or `examples/ppl` run on this model, so the per-token DRAM
  table is bytes, not tokens per second, and no kernel rate is claimed for the 1.15 b/w path.
- **The head.** §3 shows the tied F16 head dominates the served read. Until the head is stored at
  a lower rate, the projection ratio does not reach the served path.
- **The maintainer gate for `matmat.rs`.** The routing fix is in the extraction unit. The
  committed `proof/ApeironWord.lean` predates it, and `proof/corpus/Serve/Kern/R4/Matmat5.lean`
  (`fast_family`) and `R4/Matmat6.lean` (`pooled_rows`, `pooled_q8_0`, `matmat_q8act`) prove the
  bodies whose dispatch changed. `proof/regen.sh --check` and `lake build` have not been run:
  they need the pinned Aeneas/Charon and a Mathlib build that do not fit this box or the laptop.
  The change is a working-tree path, not a committed one, until that gate runs.
- **rustc and LLVM code generation, the kernel ABI, the GPU.** Out of scope as always.
