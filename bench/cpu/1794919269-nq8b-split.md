# The 8B decode split: ffn owns 56.4% of the token, and the head is not the target

Criterion: **T1**. Grade: `measured` for the split, the head rates and the step times; `model` for
the per-core instruction rate and every projection from it. Result: on the conservative Q2 pair the
decode token is **285.26 ms (3.5 tok/s)** and **ffn is the dominant term at 160.76 ms, 56.4%**, so an
ffn change is capped at **2.29×**. The shipped Q6_K head is **44.29 ms, 15.5%** — real, but 3.6×
smaller than ffn. **20 tok/s is refused by 3.4× on instructions and 1.5–2.0× on bytes.**

Command, on the i5-10210U (4C/8T, 15 W, AVX2/BMI2/POPCNT, no AVX-512, no AVX-VNNI):

```sh
systemd-run --user --scope -q -p MemoryMax=3200M -p MemorySwapMax=0 -- \
  target/release/examples/phase_split \
    --model /home/lucas/models/arelath/nq8b/skeleton-q6k-q4kembed.gguf \
    --pack /home/lucas/models/arelath/nq8b-late-v4/model.nqp --steps 24
```

Log: `bench/cpu/1794912865-nq8b-overnight-logs/K1-split.log`. `APEIRON_THREADS=4`.

Wall is 285.26 ms/token; the NQ projections (`ffn` + `attn.proj_in` + `attn.proj_out`) are
**227.68 ms, 79.9%**. `pool.wait` is an overlay and is not added. `untraced` is 0.1%, so the pass is
instrumented.

The dominant-term gate on the unmodified tree: `phase_split … --target ffn` exits 0
(`K2-target-gate.log`, `dominant=ffn`, ceiling 2.30×).

## At 2k: 2.88 tok/s, and the attention term

The same pair with a 2,048-token prompt (`generate --n 32`), three runs at 4 threads
(`K4-decode-2k.log`):

| run | prefill | decode | tok/s |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 489.754 s | 11.088 s | 2.886 |
| 2 | — | 11.003 s | 2.908 |
| 3 | — | 11.201 s | 2.857 |

**2.884 tok/s mean (346.8 ms/token), range 2.857–2.908 (1.8%).** The 62 ms/token between ctx 64 and
2k is the only position-dependent work: the attention core over 2,048 keys and the 603,979,776 B KV
read. The projections' weight reads and dots are position-independent, so they cannot explain it. The
20 tok/s gap is therefore **6.94×** on the token time, against the 5.70× the ctx-64 split implies for
the reader alone.

Prefill is 489.754 s for 2,048 tokens (0.239 s/position, 4.18 tok/s). A 1,000-token turn with that
prefill is ≈239 s, so T2's 60 s is **4.0× refused** before any decode.

## Width: the pool saturates at four participants

`run8` reran the split at 1, 2 and 8 participants (`S{1,2,8}-split.log`, 24 steps each):

| participants | wall ms/token | ffn ms | ffn share | head ms | speedup from the row above |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 655.69 | 378.285 | 57.7% | 110.885 | — |
| 2 | 355.52 | 202.502 | 57.0% | 58.678 | 1.84× |
| **4** | **285.26** | **160.760** | **56.4%** | **44.292** | **1.25×** |
| 8 | 283.20 | 158.420 | 55.9% | 36.491 | 1.01× |

The 1→2 step is 1.84×, 2→4 is 1.25×, and 4→8 is **1.007×**: the fourth participant is the last
physical core (4C/8T), and the hyperthreads buy 0.7% of the wall. `ffn`'s share is 56–58% at every
width, so the dominant term does not change and **the pool is not the lever**. The head is the one
phase that still improves at 8 (44.3 → 36.5 ms, 1.21×) because its 510,504,960 B read is
latency/bandwidth-bound rather than issue-bound; it is 13–17% of the token, so its 0.7 ms/token gain
does not move the wall. The 4-thread setting is the right one for every other row in this receipt.

## The head, on its own reader

`head_time` (K3) times the head twice — the `Exact` reference reader and the shipped `Q8Act` block
reader — and then one whole step under each:

| measure | time | rate |
|---|---:|---:|
| head, `Exact` (`lm_head_fixed`, 622,329,856 weights) | 0.534 s | 1.17 G weight/s |
| **head, `Q8Act` (shipped: `q8act_q6_k_row_fast`, 4 threads)** | **0.036 s** | **17.32 G weight/s** |
| step, `Exact` | 1.058 s | 0.94 tok/s |
| **step, `Q8Act`** | **0.280 s** | **3.58 tok/s** |

The shipped head is **14.8×** the reference head and 12.9–15.5% of the token, not the 92% the
reference reader's 0.408–0.534 s would suggest. The reference reader is 3.8× the shipped step and is
why `--kernel exact` scores at 0.5 tok/s. The head's own floor is its 510,504,960 B at the measured
read bandwidth: 23 ms at 22.33 GB/s, so a perfect head kernel saves at most 21 ms, 1.08× of the
token.

## What the numbers say about 20 tok/s

The pack holds **7,623,016,448 sign terms** per token (238,219,264 stored words × 32; the V block is
recomputed for the 72 shared sections, `speed-plan.md` §3). The NQ phases take 227.68 ms, so the
reader runs at **33.4 G terms/s aggregate, 8.36 G per core**, at the shipped `code_select_avx2` cost
of 12–14 instructions per 32 terms: **3.14 G instructions/s per core** (0.95 IPC at 3.3 GHz or
1.96 IPC at 1.6 GHz; the 15 W package does not hold turbo under four AVX2 cores).

| bound | value | vs the measured 3.5 tok/s | vs 20 tok/s |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| NQ terms/s, measured | 33.4 G | 1.00× | **5.70× short** |
| a halved instruction count (6/32), same IPC | 66.8 G → 171 ms/token | 1.67× | **3.42× short** |
| bytes/token (pack + Q6_K head; ctx 64 in this split) | 1,533,961,040 B | 5.38 GB/s used | |
| bytes/token at 2k (adds the f32 KV 603,979,776 B) | 2,137,940,816 B | 7.49 GB/s at 3.5 tok/s | 42.76 GB/s needed |
| measured read bandwidth (4 threads) | 22.33 GB/s mean, 32.06 best-pass | | **1.91× / 1.33× short** |
| spec peak | 42.656 GB/s | | **1.002× short** |

20 tok/s needs the NQ phases in **40 ms** (80% of a 50 ms token), i.e. **190.6 G terms/s**, 5.7× the
measured rate. Cutting the selector from 12 to 6 instructions buys 2× and lands at **5.84 tok/s**;
that is the best case for the one lever the dominant phase allows, and it is 3.4× short. The byte
side is a second, independent refusal: even at the 42.656 GB/s spec peak, 2.14 GB/token is 19.95
tok/s, and the measured 22.33 GB/s is 10.4 tok/s. Bytes are not binding today (5.4 GB/s used), so
the instruction stream is the first wall and the payload is the second.

**Both walls have to move.** The bytes need the head inside the pack (~80 MB) *and* a KV below f32;
the instructions need a reader near 0.17 per term, 2.3× below even a perfect 6-instruction selector.
The ternary activation reaches that arithmetic (123 G terms/s one core) and is refuted on quality
(77,741,390 PPL against 72.988013), so it is not a route.

## What this does not cover

The 200k row (the KV read and the C1 plan dominate it); the 0-shot half of Q2; rustc/LLVM codegen;
the kernel ABI; Vulkan and the GPU; the `serve/` path. The per-core instruction rate is `model` from
the source count and the measured aggregate; it is not a hardware-counter reading (`perf` is
`perf_event_paranoid=4` on this host). The 1- and 2-thread 2k runs were cut from K4: a 1-thread
prefill is 30+ minutes for a decode width `phase_split` measures in 25 s; the width sweep in the
section above is at ctx 64, where the decode is 25 s. The 4-thread columns here are the 4-participant
ones.
