# nq: the pooled projection keeps its scratch planes and writes its output columns in place

Criterion: **T2** (turn latency), partial: the cold 1,827-position prefill of the 0.6B pack at block
1024, and its turn with 64 decode tokens. It is not a 1,000-token turn at 200k, and not a 7B result.
Grades: `measured` for the cold walls, the phase split, the faults and RSS, the box decode split and
the quoted llama.cpp baseline; `tested` for the pooled equality tests, including the allocating-arm
equality. Machine: OVH box `ubuntu@139.99.223.161` (Sydney; 8 vCPU EPYC Milan, 8 sockets × 1 core ×
1 thread, 31.3 GiB, 256 MiB L3, no cpufreq governor, no AVX-512, no VNNI), no GPU. Times UTC. The
laptop, the only device a timed claim may name, is **not measured**; every wall below is box
`measured` (Omissions).

Model: `~/models/qwen3-0.6b-base-nq/nq-pack/skeleton-q8head.gguf`; pack
`~/models/nq-scratch/nq06-v3.nqp` (196 linears, 28 layers, 0.977441 b/w); harness
`examples/prefix_solve.rs`, `--len 1827 --differ 205 --decode 0 --mb 440`. Every run under
`~/bin/adg run --` (agent-guard, 16 GiB cap, 8 jobs, printed as the log's first line);
`touch src/lib.rs` before the timed builds.

## What changed

`src/engine/kern/nq.rs`, two removals from the serial path around each pooled projection; the
arithmetic, the readers and the loop nestings are untouched.

**1. The two stage planes are the calling thread's across calls.** `Planes` retains `t` (`k · inn`
pre-scaled activation words) and `z` (`k · rank` intermediate words) in a thread-local, and `cover`
grows a plane only when a call is wider than every earlier one; `grow_to` reserves the exact
shortfall before extending, so a doubling `resize` cannot leave a capacity above the length asked
for. Both planes were `vec![0; …]` per call: an `mmap` and its zeroing on the dispatching thread
before each pool pass. The body writes every word of both before a stage reads it, so a retained
plane needs no zeroing. `PLANE_CAP` is 32 MiB per plane (4 Mi words): the 0.6B's widest `down` plane
at a 1,024-column band is 24 MiB and is retained; the 7B's `down` plane at the same band is 148 MiB
(155 MB) and takes the allocating arm for that call, so the retained set stays inside the byte
budget.

**2. The `U` stage writes each output column into its `Vec`.** The stage filled a flat
`y: vec![0; k · out]` and the readout then copied `y` into `k` fresh vectors, both on the dispatching
thread after the pool pass; the flat plane was zeroed before the stage overwrote every word of it.
At the `ffn` width (out 3,072) and a 1,024-column band the plane is **24 MiB**: a 24 MiB memset and a
24 MiB read plus write per band, 196 linears per band, while every worker sat at the barrier. The
column vectors are now sized inside the pool task that fills them, so their allocation and zeroing
moved off the dispatching thread, and no column is copied after the stage.

|---|---:|---:|---:|

Peak RSS moves **+85,884 KiB (+83.9 MiB, +6.7%)**, from 1.229 GiB to
**1.311 GiB**. This changes M1 peak accounting at this length; it is not neutral. The
retained pair is at most 30 MiB on a thread that runs `down` (`t` 1,024 × 3,072 words = 24 MiB, `z`
1,024 × 736 words = 6 MiB), carried by every thread that calls the pooled entry, and the rest is not
separated: moving `col.resize(self.out, 0)` into the pool tasks moves their allocation and zeroing
into worker arenas (glibc keeps freed task memory per arena), which is a plausible carrier of the
remainder but is `model`, not measured. Minor faults move +7.7%: the flat output plane's pages are replaced
by the per-column vectors' pages, faulted inside the pool tasks, and the retained planes grow once
through the band shapes.

## The claiming pool, ported and not kept

llama.cpp's GEMM does not give each participant a fixed stride: after `ggml_barrier` every
participant starts on its own index and takes the next with `ggml_threadpool_chunk_add(1)`
(`ggml/src/ggml-cpu/llamafile/sgemm.cpp:596-627`; the counter is
`ggml/src/ggml-cpu/ggml-cpu.c:617`, and the same protocol is in `ggml/src/ggml-cpu/repack.cpp:4376`
and `ops.cpp:9132`). That loop was ported into `chart::pool::run_partition` and built as
`~/bin/prefix_solve.claim`, against `~/bin/prefix_solve.inter` — the same tree and kernel with only
`pool.rs` differing — three interleaved pairs per width (`/tmp/opencode/ab-claim.log`):

| participants | stride s | claim s | claim/stride |
|:---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 42.535 | 42.111 | 0.990 |
| 4 | 14.440 | 14.362 | 0.995 |
| 8 | 10.721 | 10.832 | 1.010 |

Every delta is inside its width's run spread (2.2%, 1.2% and 2.1%), and the directions disagree
between widths, so the port was reverted and the pool keeps its stride. `model`: this pool
publishes the epoch and runs its own partition immediately, without llama.cpp's barrier, so the
caller can take indices a parked worker would have served; llama.cpp touches the counter only with
every participant awake, so its balance does not transfer without that handshake. The hosting
kernel (the interleaved tile) was itself dropped when its equality test failed, so these rows are
not measurements of the committed kernel.

`the_allocating_arm_matches_the_retained_arm` clears the thread's `PLANES`, then runs the cap-0 call
before the retained call at each width, so a cap test that took the retained arm would leave the
planes non-empty and fail the length assertion; the widths descend `33, 7, 1` and the activation
words depend on `k`, so a stage that leaves a word inside its prefix unwritten shows as a difference
from the single-column oracle. It also calls a cap that straddles `z_len` and `t_len`, which must
allocate, and `project_pooled_shape` with emptied planes, which must retain `(k · inn, k · rank)`.
`grow_to_reserves_exactly_the_length` asserts `capacity() == len` after each growth. The retained
arm's reuse across calls is `the_pooled_block_is_bit_equal_at_every_block`'s. The falsifier is
`/tmp/opencode/mutation.sh`: with the cap test forced `if true`, the allocating-arm test fails at
`cap 0 must not grow the thread's planes` (`left: (0, 0)`, `right: (957, 759)`; `mutated-exit=101`);
restored, it passes (`restored-exit=0`), and the source md5 is `bd5e02dd…` before and after
(`/tmp/opencode/mutation.log`).

## Review

Reviews ran on the corrected receipt and kernel: `agy --model gemini-4-hydrogen-high`, logs
`/tmp/opencode/review-planes.log` and `/tmp/opencode/review-planes2.log`. The first refused a tree
hash mismatch between the gated binary and the working tree; a `git show` command attributed to the
box, which has no `.git`; a quality statement resting only on a synthetic fixture; a false "M1
accounting is unchanged" sentence; the single-run `norm`/`residual` phase rows; a ceiling quoted for
`ffn` alone without the three-leaf share; `PLANE_CAP` bounding length but not a doubling `Vec`
capacity; and a test that could not falsify `cover`'s truncation. The second refused the RSS and
faults table's misattribution to a log that never ran `/usr/bin/time -v` and a build without
`reserve_exact`; the claim-pool width-1 arithmetic; a `llama-bench` command that ran only `-t 8`;
decode widths 1 and 4 timed on an earlier build; the allocating-arm test's order (`retained` first),
its `k`-independent words, the missing straddle cap, the untested `project_pooled_shape` cap and
`grow_to` capacity; the `residual` row described as noise; the missing next-target phase; and the
baseline note's absent logs, stddevs, command and window labels. This version fixes each: every row
is the shipped `bd5e02dd…` source timed by one `ship-gate` run, the test is reordered and widened,
the RSS is re-measured on `final5`, the claim row reads 42.535 / 0.990, the note carries logs,
stddevs and the exact command, and the next prefill target is named above.

## Omissions

- The laptop is not measured. Every wall here is box `measured`; the box is 8 × EPYC Milan with
  256 MiB L3 and no VNNI, the laptop is 4 × i5-10210U with 6 MiB L3, so none of these ratios is a
  laptop claim and no "model for the laptop" is asserted.
- The split between the retained planes and the in-place `U` stage is not measured. Both remove
  serial work between pool passes and were kept as one change; the flat plane's 24 MiB memset and
  copy dominate the arithmetic. The sub-steps inside `ffn` / `project_pooled_body` (weight read,
  decode, dot, `t`/`z` pack, scratch allocation, `U` write) are not timed separately.
- The allocating arm is `tested` equal to the retained arm and not timed. A shape above 32 MiB per
  plane takes it; the 7B's `down` plane does.
- Quality is not re-measured with `examples/ppl`: the change is scratch reuse and output placement
  over the same readers and accumulation order. The evidence is `tested` bit equality
  (`the_pooled_block_is_bit_equal_at_every_block` and the allocating-arm test) plus the end-to-end
  first token, which is 315 in every 1,827-token run and 15,678 in every
  `205 + 64` run
  across both arms and all blocks.
- Block 256 and the other block widths are not re-run; this A/B is at the default block 1024.
  `bench/cpu/1795573862-prefill-block-and-group.md` measured the block axis.
- The 7B pack, the 200k context and the long-context turn are not run: the pack is not on this box.
- The retained set is bounded per thread, not per process: two planes at `PLANE_CAP` are 64 MiB, so
  eight calling threads could hold 512 MiB. Only threads that run a pooled projection hold a pair,
  and the 0.6B measured +83.9 MiB, but the 7B's worst case on the 6.6 GiB laptop is an M1 input this
  receipt does not measure.
- The HEAD binary was built in an earlier session from the committed source held on the box as
  `/tmp/nq.head.rs` (md5 `b9a1004f…`); the box has no `.git` and did not rebuild it for this run.
  Both arms ran interleaved on the same box in the same session.
- `~/bin/prefix_solve.old` (an earlier session's binary) is not a HEAD build: it is byte-identical
  to `~/bin/prefix_solve.rev`, built from the working tree before `0f65d137` restored the padded
  `shift_vecs` form. No row above uses it as a baseline.
