# The 1.18 b/w selection: three late layers measure 1.499× the stock Q4 file on window 1

Criterion: **Q2**. Grade: `measured` for every PPL and byte count; `anchor` for the energy-to-nats
mapping and the depth law, both stated before the runs and tested by them.

**Superseded in scope, not in number.** Window 1 measures 1.499225× (0.0517% under the bar) and
window 2 1.467112× (2.1925% under), both `measured`. The five-window pooled estimate is **1.499754**
with a 95% interval of [1.391674, 1.616228] — the pack sits on the bar and the criterion is
**undecided**; see `bench/cpu/1794922136-nq8b-q2-five-windows.md`. The rows below remain the window-1/2
record and the layer-selection evidence. Two planes on 15 sections of layers 33–35 measure **11.374839 PPL,
1.499225×** against 7.587145 with the stock **Q6_K** `output.weight` and **Q4_K**
`token_embd.weight` installed; all **147** common tensor types and dimensions match the stock
Q4_K_M file, its two quantised tensors are byte-identical to it, and only the 252 fitted linears
differ in function (`bench/cpu/1794921128-nq8b-tensor-audit.md`). The 1.5× bar is 11.3807175 and the margin is
**0.005878 PPL (0.0517%)**. With the Q8_0 embedding the same pack measures 11.346170 (1.495447×)
and with the original Q8_0 head 11.294969 (1.488698×); both are less conservative and are not the
Q2 number. The spend is 7.971% of the weights for **0.076220 nats**, 2.34× the full residual's
average 0.2041 nats/b/w and above the 2.10× the bar required. The late/early sensitivity ratio is
8.92× in nats per unit of removed error energy. The harder second window raises the Q4 arm's nll by **0.444528** and the pack's by **0.422875**, so
the ratio improves to **1.467112** (**17.361910** against **11.834074**): the text difficulty is close
to additive in nats, not proportional. Both arms ran on this laptop, and the Q4 window-1 run reproduces
the rented host's 7.587145 to the last digit, so the window-1 ratio is no longer cross-host. The
0-shot half is open and is the only part of Q2 still unmeasured. The overnight state, queue and
artifacts are `bench/cpu/1794912865-nq8b-overnight.md`.

The reallocation this receipt predicted next is refuted by three controls. One plane on the same 15
late sections measures **11.842884, 1.560914×**, 0.028850 nats: the first plane buys 38% of the
pair's gain for 50% of its bytes, and the second plane's incremental marginal is **1.64×** the
first's per byte. One plane on 37 sections of layers 30–35 at the same bytes measures **12.687998,
1.672302×**, 0.040080 nats **worse than no residual**. The same 37 sections at two planes measure
**11.080537, 1.460436×** at 1.339927 b/w, so the section set is fine at the pair and the one-plane
failure is the plane count; the pair is **0.159927 b/w over the cap**, a control and not a product.
The source pack shows why one plane is not half the pair: plane 1's sign differs from plane 0's on
**58.08%** of all weights, so the pair is a cancellation code. The pre-run statement was an anchor
extrapolation and is withdrawn; `tools/arm_admit.py` refuses the arm.

The r1late selection is the `--rank energy` greedy at the one-plane cost: 37 sections on layers
30–35, 17.7027% of `E0`, 16.06% of the weights. The earlier planning row said 41 sections and
21.3227%; that was a different selection and is corrected here. The pack built is the one in
`bench/cpu/1794896874-nq8b-depth-logs/r1late-sections.csv`.

## The residual energy model, and what it does not say

Each plane stores `α_l,i = mean|R_l,i|` as a bfloat16 row scale. Under `R ~ N(0, σ²)`, `α = σ√(2/π)`
and one plane leaves `(1 − 2/π)σ² = 0.363σ²`. The measured ratio of the two planes' scales over all
252 sections is `E1/E0 = 0.3777`, so plane 0 removes **62.2%** of the error energy and plane 1 37.8%
of `E0`: the pair leaves `0.1427 E0`, an **85.7%** removal.

The nats-per-energy coefficient is pinned by the one measured full point: two planes on every
section remove `0.857 E0` and buy `2.500577 − 2.091134 = 0.409444` nats, so `0.4777` nats per unit
of `E0`. A selection that covers energy share `f` with **the pair** buys `0.857·f·0.4777` nats.

That coefficient is measured for the pair and does not decompose per plane. The sequential fit
determines plane 1 from `R_1 = R_0 − P_0`, so plane 1's sign is opposite plane 0's wherever plane 0
overshot, `|R_0| < α_0`. For normal `R_0` that is `2Φ(0.798) − 1 = 57.5%` of the weights. The v3
pack measures **58.08%** overall, 57.90% on layers 33–35 and 60.19% on layers 0–2. The pair is a
cancellation code: on 58% of the weights plane 1 pulls plane 0 back. The 62.2% is an energy
decomposition of the pair, not the share of the pair's *loss value* that plane 0 carries alone.

The late1 control measures the split directly:

| plane | energy removed | nats | nats per `E0` | nats/bw |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 5.5343% | 0.028850 | 0.5213 | 0.3615 |
| 1, increment | 2.0909% | 0.047370 | 2.2655 | 0.5936 |
| pair | 7.6251% | 0.076220 | 0.9996 | 0.4776 |

Plane 1 removes 27% of the pair's energy and buys 62% of its nats. The energy order is reversed in
loss. This is the input the failed prediction assumed away.

## Admission rule

`tools/arm_admit.py` reads `bench/cpu/1794896874-nq8b-depth-arms.csv` and classifies an arm:

- **decision**: an exact repeat of a measured arm (same layers, kinds and plane count). Only this
  class may carry a Q2 claim; a new allocation is not a decision however good the model looks.
- **control**: exactly one axis outside the measured domain. It may run, with a registered reading;
  it may not be quoted as a product result. late2, early2, late1 and r1late2 are controls.
- **refuse**: two axes outside the domain, or a one-plane arm that also leaves the measured block.
  r1late is refused; the tool exits 1. A one-plane arm carries the sign warning in every case: the
  pair is a cancellation code, so plane 0 alone is a control at best and never a decision.

`--audit` replays the recorded arms; `--self-test` removes the measured late block and requires
late1 to turn from control to refuse, so the check can fail. The sign check is the measured input
the model lacked: it reads the plane words from the v3 pack and counts where plane 1 differs from
plane 0. The arm table is `bench/cpu/1794896874-nq8b-depth-arms.csv`.

## The same-quantisation comparison

Q2 compares the pack with Q4_K_M on the same non-linear tensors. The original NQ skeleton's
`output.weight` and `token_embd.weight` are both `Q8_0`; the stock file's are `Q6_K` and `Q4_K`.
The pack replaces only the 252 linears, so those two tensors come from the skeleton. Two compact
skeletons were built from the stock file's own tensors, and the same pack was rescored:

The same-quantisation skeleton matches the stock Q4_K_M file on all **147** common tensor names,
types and dimensions, and its two quantised tensors — `token_embd.weight` (Q4_K, 350,060,544 B) and

## What the depth axis says

The pair's late arm buys **0.076220 nats** with 0.159600 b/w of residual (2 bits on 7.971% of the
weights), **0.4776 nats/b/w** against the full two-plane pack's 0.2041 — **2.34×** the average. The
energy share of those sections is 8.8975%, so the energy model predicted 0.036430 nats and the
measurement is 2.09× it. Depth is not an energy effect: the late layers' fit error is only 1.116×
per byte the average, while their nats per unit error are 1.87× the average. The same-layer block
is 1/12 of the model, so the selection is 2.34× on 8% of the weights, not a global property.

The early arm tests the other side with the same bytes and kinds. It measures **0.003890 nats**
against the energy model's 0.016594, a factor of **0.234** — the mirror image of the late arm's
2.09. The two blocks are 33 layers apart, so the sensitivity ratio is **8.92×** in nats per unit of
removed error energy:

| block | energy share | nats/b/w | sensitivity factor |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| layers 0–2 | 4.0528% | 0.0244 | 0.234 |
| layers 33–35 | 8.8975% | 0.4776 | 2.09 |

The early pack is 12.142202, 1.600365×, only 0.0039 nats better than v2 for the same 0.16 b/w —
0.12× the average marginal. An error injected at layer 0 is washed out by 33 blocks; an error
injected at layer 35 reaches the head through one. That is the axis that pays.

## What the plane axis says

The pair is not two independent half-bits. At the same 15 late sections, plane 0 alone buys
0.028850 nats and the pair buys 0.076220; plane 1's increment is **0.047370 nats**, 1.64× plane 0's
per byte, for 27% of the pair's removed energy. The sign bits explain the order: plane 1 disagrees
with plane 0 on 58.08% of the weights, so it spends most of its code cancelling plane 0's overshoot,
and the pair is fitted as a unit. The energy split (62.2/37.8) is the right split for *energy* and
the wrong split for *loss*.

That is why r1late failed. One plane on 37 sections removes 11.01% of `E0` under the anchor and
scores −0.040080 nats. At the measured plane-0 sensitivity of the 15 late sections (0.5213 nats/E0)
the same energy would predict +0.0574 nats (11.51); the arm is 0.0975 nats below that and its sign
is wrong. The 2×2 isolates the axis:

| sections | 1 plane | 2 planes |
|---|---:|---:|
| 15 (33–35 × five) | 11.842884, +0.028850 nats, 1.098820 b/w | 11.294969, +0.076220 nats, 1.178785 b/w |
| 37 (30–35 × seven) | 12.687998, −0.040080 nats, 1.179391 b/w | 11.080537, +0.095387 nats, 1.339927 b/w |

Adding the 22 sections to the pair buys **+0.019167 nats** for +0.161143 b/w, **0.119 nats/bw** or
0.25× the late block's 0.4776. Adding them to plane 0 **costs 0.068929 nats** for +0.080571 b/w,
**−0.86 nats/bw**. The section set is not the failure; the plane count is. The pair on 37 sections
passes the 1.5× bar at **1.460436×**, 0.300181 PPL below it, but is **0.159927 b/w over the cap**,
so it is a control, not a product.

## What the failure was

The prediction had one measured coefficient (0.4777 nats/E0 from the full two-plane pack), one
anchor (the 0.622/0.857 plane split read from stored scales, never a scored one-plane arm), and a
two-parameter depth law fitted through two arms with no residual degrees of freedom. It applied all
three to a new plane count and a new section set:

- The depth law was measured before the run and showed an 8.92× spread. The r1late prediction used
  the average coefficient, so it ignored the very measurement that had just falsified the average
  for late layers; the working note's 11.025591 also dropped the 0.622 factor.
- The predictions table described a 41-section, 21.3227%-energy selection; the pack built is 37
  sections at 17.7027%. A prediction for an arm that was not built is not a prediction of the run.
  The 37-section set is not the cause of the result: at two planes it measures 11.080537, better
  than the 15-section pair; the one-plane code is the cause.
- The source pack contained the refuting measurement at zero cost: 58.08% sign disagreement between
  the planes. It was not read. A joint two-bit code is not a nested prefix, and the 62.2% energy
  share does not transfer to the first bit alone.
- The numbers were quoted to six decimals, which dressed an extrapolation as a measurement. The
  admissible statement was a refusal, not a point.

The fix is executable, not another coefficient: an arm is a decision only if it repeats a measured
arm exactly; any new allocation is a control with a registered reading; a one-plane arm is checked
against the source pack's sign bits and refused when the pair is a cancellation code. That is the
same rule as the rest of the tree — an `anchor` does not authorise a run or a claim.
