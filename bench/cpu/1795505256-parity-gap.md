# Where the 3.8× is: the parity budget at 1,827 positions

Criterion: **T2** (turn latency), partial. No T1: decode is not timed in this receipt. Grades:
`measured` for the phase times, the term counts that close against a served pack, and the rates
quoted from `1794919269-nq8b-split.md`; `model` for every division that attributes a leaf to its
arithmetic.

Model: `skeleton-q8head.gguf` + `nq06-v3.nqp` (0.977441 b/w, 53,815,472 B, 418,381,824 sign
terms/token over 196 linears, ranks q/o 640, k/v 480, gate/up/down 736). llama.cpp `049326a` on
`Qwen3-0.6B-Base.f16.gguf` (1,198,182,592 B). Machine: i5-10210U, 4 cores, 6.6 GiB, `powersave`.
The engine figures are the tiled F32 run in `/tmp/opencode/nq-attn-phase.log` (cold 80.254 s,
43.93 ms/token); the comparison run's wall is 46.93 ms/token. llama's cold prompt eval in the same
window is 11.69 ms/token.

## The cold budget, and the 32.2 ms gap

llama's per-token counted work is 0.6 G projection MACs and 0.105 G attention MACs, 1.41 GFLOP in
11.69 ms = **120.6 GFLOP/s aggregate**. Splitting that rate by work: projections **9.94 ms**,
attention **1.74 ms**.

| leaf | measured ms/token | share | llama, same work at 120.6 GFLOP/s | gap |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `attn.core` | 15.44 | 35.1% | 1.74 | **13.7** |
| projections (`ffn` 16.42 + `proj_in` 7.83 + `proj_out` ~3.1) | 27.35 | 62.3% | 9.94 | **17.4** |
| norm, residual, head, pass remainder | ~1.14 | 2.6% | ~0 | 1.1 |
| **total** | **43.93** | | **11.69** | **32.2** |

Attention owns **43%** of the gap, the projection leaves **54%**, the rest 3%. The per-leaf rates in
the receipt (`1795503411`) are measured; the llama split is `model`, since llama has no phase trace.

## Attention runs at 13.6 GFLOP/s, 8.8× below llama's aggregate rate

383 GFLOP of attention in 28.204 s. The kernel issues, per score: one 16-FMA dependent chain over
`head_dim = 128` (one 8-lane accumulator), two scalar `exp_f32_scalar` calls, then 16 more dependent
FMAs for the value accumulator. The cold prefill evaluates 748,105,344 scores and **1.496 G scalar
exponentials**; at the measured 28.204 s that is 37.7 ns of wall per score, or **241 core-cycles per
score** at the 1.6 GHz the 15 W part holds under four AVX2 cores (`1794919269`'s measured
assumption) against an instruction count near 90 — about **0.38 IPC**. The kernel is latency-bound,
not issue-bound: the 16-deep FMA chain is 64 cycles before the serial polynomial `exp`, then 64 more
for the value accumulator, and no second score is in flight inside a query.

llama's flash attention keeps the same MAC count but runs 8 f32 lanes of `ggml_v_expf` over a query
tile, so the same 128-term dot and the same exponential occupy a fraction of the cycles. Our tile
kernel already batches 32 queries per K/V row; what it does not batch is the elementary function.
The word-equality contract forbids reordering a query's own sum, but it does not forbid any of:

- skip the `alpha` exponential when `score <= m_prev`: `exp(0)` is exactly 1 and
  `fma(1, w·v) == w·v + a`, so the words are unchanged;
- evaluate 8 queries' `w = exp(score − m_next)` in one vector with the same polynomial and the same
  per-lane rounding (Rust does not contract `p * r + c`, so `_mm256_mul_ps`+`_mm256_add_ps` matches);
- interleave 4 independent queries' FMA chains, one accumulator each.

## The projections run at 15.3 G terms/s; the same kernel measures 33.4 G/s

418,381,824 sign terms in 27.35 ms is **15.3 G terms/s**. The shipped `code_select_avx2` reader
measures **33.4 G terms/s aggregate, 8.36 G per core** on the 8B shapes
(`1794919269-nq8b-split.md`: 7.62 G terms in 227.68 ms). At that rate the 0.6B projection floor is
**12.5 ms/token**, 22.9 s of the cold turn, so the fixture is **2.2× off its own kernel**.

The 0.6B linears are short: `inn` 1,024 and `rank` 480–736 are 15–32 words per row, against 64–128
on the 8B. The pooled loop is rank-row outer, column inner, so each column's four selector planes
are re-read once per rank row. They fit L2, not L1, and the fixed cost of the horizontal fold and
the per-row epilogue is spread over 4–8× fewer terms than on the 8B.

Unlike attention, the sign dot sums integers exactly, so block over rank rows and columns freely:
every dot is independent and every partial sum is exact. Only the per-column and per-row
`mul_q32` epilogues have a fixed order, and they sit outside the blocking.

### The exact sign dot cannot reach llama's floor; the Q8 reader can

`sign_dot_avx2`/`sign_dot_planes_avx2` spend 12–14 instructions per 32 terms because `t` is a Q32.32
`i64` coefficient: its magnitude needs four 8-bit digit planes, each one `vpmaddubsw` + `vpmaddwd` +
`vpaddd` (`1794811020-one-bit-vs-four-ceiling.md`). llama.cpp's prefill does the opposite: it
quantises the *activation* to `q8_0` and runs `ggml_vec_dot_q8_0_q8_0`, one `vpmaddubsw` +
`vpmaddwd` per 16 weights.

`Reader::Q8` in `src/engine/kern/nq.rs` is that structure already: `code_select_avx2` builds a 0/1
selector from the sign word with three shuffles and folds it against the int8 codes as
`vpmaddubsw`+`vpmaddwd`+`vpsllvd`+`vpaddd` — 7 vector ops per 32 terms, not 12–14, and the codes are
one byte instead of four digit planes. At equal IPC that is **1.7× the exact reader**, i.e. a
projection floor near **7.4 ms/token**, below llama's 9.94. The ratio is `model`: it is counted from
the two bodies, not timed here.

The cost is the words. A q8 activation is not the exact Q32.32 word, so `project_q8_pooled` is a
carrier change and needs a PPL row on the same token stream before any speed claim on the product
path. That is llama.cpp's own trade at every K-quant GEMM, and it is the only lever found so far that
puts the 1-bit pack *below* f16 on prefill arithmetic.

## What this means for parity

At the kernel's own measured rate the projection floor is 12.5 ms/token against llama's 9.94. **The
1-bit pack is 1.26× more arithmetic per prefill token than llama's f16 path even at its floor, and
2.75× in the fixture as it runs.** Its advantage is 2.25× fewer bytes per token, which is a decode
property. Parity at 1,827 therefore needs both:

1. The projection at its own floor (12.5 ms instead of 27.35): **−14.9 ms/token**.
2. Attention at llama's per-FLOP rate (3–4 ms instead of 15.44): **−11 to −12 ms/token**.

Together those put the cold turn at **17.1 ms/token** (43.93 − 14.85 − 11.9), against llama's
11.69, 1.46×; closing the last 5.4 ms needs the short-linear floor below 12.5, i.e. more than 33.4 G
terms/s or a lower-rank allocation. The tail has the same two terms (attention 28.6 ms of 59.18,
projections ~28.6): the same two changes put it at **18.0 ms** against llama's 15.76, 1.14×.

## Priorities

1. **Attention exponentials (exact).** First the `alpha` skip, then an 8-lane `w` exponential over
   the query tile. Gate: `test_attn_core_query_tile_equals_the_per_query_core`,
   `the_tile_group_read_equals_the_per_column_group_read`,
   `the_attention_tile_task_equals_the_per_column_task`, then a same-binary A/B. Largest single
   term, and the tail's dominant leaf.
2. **Projection blocking for short linears.** Rank-row blocking so each selector plane read serves
   several rows; target 15.3 → 30 G terms/s. Exact by integer associativity; gate is the existing
   bit-equality of pooled against per-column projection.
3. **The Q8 activation reader** (`project_q8_pooled`) once blocking lands: 1.7× the exact reader by
   instruction count, below llama's floor, but it changes the words and needs a PPL row first.
4. Re-run the split. If both reach their floors the next term is the F16 head on decode
   (155.7M weights/token) and the norm/residual elementwise pass.

## What this does not cover

- The 0.6B is a fixture. Its 196 short linears are not the 7B's 28 large ones; the 33.4 G/s rate is
  the 8B's, and no 7B pack has been served here.
- The phase-to-arithmetic division is `model`, not a measured per-instruction attribution. The
  `exp` share is inferred from the instruction budget and the score count, not from a counter.
- Quality is not addressed: this pack scores 74.3 PPL at ctx 512 against Q4_K_M's 18.12 on the same
  token stream, so parity on this artifact is a fixture result.
- Every absolute time here is from the loaded window (89 °C, load 3.7–7.5); the shares and the
  term counts are stable, the milliseconds are not.
